Ship Engine Anomaly Detection

A statistical rule and two unsupervised models compared for flagging abnormal ship-engine sensor readings, as candidates for engineering review.

Type
Anomaly Detection
Domain
Maritime Operations
Methods
IQR, One-Class SVM, Isolation Forest
Origin
Cambridge DS Career Accelerator coursework
Code
GitHub repository

The Challenge

An IQR rule flagged 422 of 19,535 ship-engine readings (2.16%) where at least two sensors were out of range, giving engineers a short list of readings to review.

Engine faults are expensive at sea, and sensor data arrives faster than engineers can review it. With no labelled faults, the question was how to flag a manageable set of unusual readings, and how statistical and machine-learning methods compare.

Approach

01
Data review
19,535 readings from six sensors (engine speed, lubricating-oil pressure and temperature, fuel pressure, coolant pressure and temperature); no missing or duplicate rows.
02
Statistical rule
Interquartile-range (IQR) bounds per sensor; a reading is flagged when at least two sensors are out of range at once.
03
Unsupervised models
One-Class SVM and Isolation Forest on standardised sensor values, at several settings.
04
Visual check
PCA used to plot the flagged readings in two dimensions.

Results

Isolation Forestcontamination = 0.05977 (5.0%)

The IQR rule is the only result not fixed in advance. For One-Class SVM and Isolation Forest, the nu and contamination settings decide roughly what share of readings is flagged, so their 5% figures reflect the chosen setting rather than a discovery. Readings flagged by several methods are the strongest candidates for review.

Limitations: no labelled faults, so none of the methods can be scored for accuracy; readings are treated independently, without time order.
