Statistical Analysis

Statistical Hypothesis Testing Framework

Six business questions, each answered with a test chosen to fit the data: two-sample t-test, one-way ANOVA, chi-square and Mann-Whitney U. Normality was checked before any parametric test was trusted, and every result was translated into a plain-language recommendation.

Type
Statistical Analysis
Domain
Retail, HR, Marketing, Manufacturing
Methods
t-test, ANOVA, Chi-square, Mann-Whitney U
Origin
Cambridge DS Career Accelerator coursework

The Challenge

Six business questions, six tests chosen to fit the data. Three of the six nulls were retained, including a competitor pricing comparison (p = 0.151) and a two-campaign engagement comparison (p = 0.528). Reporting "no evidence of a difference" is what stops noise being sold as an effect.

Each scenario came with a decision attached: whether a store's prices differ from a competitor's, whether productivity differs across three departments, whether age group relates to preferred social media platform, whether defect rates differ across three production lines, which book category earns more revenue, and which of two marketing campaigns drives more customer engagement.

The first five scenarios used small sample datasets supplied with each brief. The campaign comparison used a 200-record engagement dataset, 100 customers per campaign.

Approach

01
Problem Framing
Stated a null and alternative hypothesis for each scenario before running any test, with α = 0.05 throughout.
02
Test Selection
Matched the test to the data: independent two-sample t-test for two group means, one-way ANOVA for three or more, chi-square test of independence for categorical counts.
03
Assumption Checking
For the campaign data, checked normality with histograms, Q-Q plots and Shapiro-Wilk. Both groups failed (p = 0.001 and p = 0.003), so the non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test replaced the t-test.
04
Reporting
Translated each result into a short recommendation for the stakeholder, stating whether the evidence supports acting.

Results

6 Tests
Pricing, productivity, social media, quality control, revenue and campaign engagement
3 of 6
Nulls retained: pricing (p = 0.151), social media (p = 0.441, sample too small to conclude) and campaign engagement (p = 0.528)
p = 0.004
Defect rates differ across three production lines (10%, 15% and 5%; chi-square = 11.11)

Rejected nulls: productivity differs across sales, marketing and finance (one-way ANOVA, F = 143.0, p < 0.001); defect rates differ across three production lines (chi-square = 11.11, p = 0.004); non-fiction titles earn higher mean revenue than fiction (F = 92.5, p < 0.001; with two groups this is equivalent to an independent t-test).

Retained nulls: no significant price difference between the store and its competitor (t = -1.48, p = 0.151), and no significant engagement difference between Campaign A and Campaign B (Mann-Whitney U = 4,741, p = 0.528).

Retrospective note: the social media scenario ran a chi-square test on only five observations, well below the minimum expected count of five per cell that the test needs. Its result (p = 0.441) cannot support any conclusion either way; Fisher's exact test or a larger sample is the right route.
